### One pair of scenarios describing a change in one or more forest sector practices 
For each study, pairs of variables were recorded to describe the change in carbon sequestration and/or emission between two scenarios. These carbon budget comparisons were assigned to one change in a forest sector practice such as silviculture, supply chain, or technology responsible for the assessed change in carbon balance, and called a **driver**. The core data of each comparison consists of the values of the carbon balance for the two scenarios, defined by a ‘reference’ and ‘alternative’ driver configuration leading to a reference (Cref) and alternative (Calt) carbon sequestration and their respective amounts of wood harvested (Qwoodref) and (Qwoodalt). The change in carbon sequestration in each record is attributed to a change in an actual biological, sociological or economic driver. For each record (i.e. pairs of scenarios), only the driver that explains the change in carbon balance is recorded, and not all drivers evaluated in the paper, so that all drivers’ effects on C balance quantified in a paper were recorded as separate records.

### Methodological approaches behind each pair of scenarios
Each carbon budget comparison had several associated metadata, characterizing the information needed for the analysis and for transparency of the database. Metadata included: 
1. The geographical scale and location of the study, where scale could be world, region, country, city, stand, product and location pointing to the country or multi-country region of location for scales smaller or equal to country. If two countries are involved in the study, we recorded the upper administrative unit of both countries.
2. The time frame of the study was recorded with start and end years or alternatively duration only if no start and end year were provided
3. The component of the wood sector addressed in the recorded comparison was recorded by selecting types of products affected by the driver change in the study among “Upstream”, “Timber”, “Pulp and paper”, “Energy”, or “Economy_sector” when the study computed a carbon budget going beyond the wood sector (e.g., energy production including a mix of feedstocks, wood being one of them but never the only one)
4. Processes and their boundaries accounted for in carbon estimates were recorded by searching the methodological section of the publication for the presence of key carbon balance components accounted for: soil carbon, harvest residues, live biomass, carbon products storage, end-of-life biogenic carbon emissions, fossil emissions due to forestry, manufacturing, maintenance and end-of-life processes
5. The explicit accounting of the time dimension both with respect to in-situ sequestration (forest dynamics and biogenic decay varying over time), fossil emission processes (burning/recycling varying over time), market-based mechanisms exogenous (LUC) or endogenous (rebound effects) was recorded as well
6. Finally, use of displacement factors was recorded as well as displacement factor references if they were extracted from another publication.

### Consistent definition of the reference scenario
For each pair, the scenario with the lowest value for wood consumption was chosen as the reference, which, when unknown, was assumed to be the option with least harvest, least efficient processes, slowest-growing species least recycling, or least fertile conditions. When more than two scenarios were displayed for a given driver (for example three levels of intensity), one of the scenarios was defined as the reference and all other values were considered as alternative scenarios and compared to the reference as a separate pair, i.e., in a separate record in the table. When several scenarios were displayed in the form of a sensitivity analysis, expert judgment was used to record  few data pairs based on the redundancy of the results. In particular, when the driver’s effect was roughly linear, only the most extreme scenarios were extracted. When results were displayed separately for different combinations of processes  (for example, LCA emissions and in-situ biogenic C balance are both displayed explicitly), both were retained  as separate records and differentiated by the meta-data on the processes that were accounted for. Carbon variables were extracted from tables when possible and when graphical extraction from figures was needed, the extraction was performed using the online WebPlotDigitizer tool.
